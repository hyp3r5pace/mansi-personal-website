Vinod has been carving teak printing blocks for thirty-one years. His
workshop sits behind a courtyard in Sanganer, three rooms deep, and the
sound when you walk in is the soft, regular thud of carved wood meeting
cotton — a metronome the family has been keeping for two generations.

I went to commission a small set of blocks for the spring capsule and came
back with a notebook full of things I hadn't thought to ask about. We sat
on the printing floor for two hours.

## On rhythm

"The block doesn't make the cloth," he told me. "The rhythm does. If you
press the same way every time, the cloth is dull. If your hand changes —
even a little — the cloth breathes."

I'd been thinking about block-print as a print problem. Vinod thinks about
it as a music problem.

> A print that is too perfect is not a print. It is a photograph of one.

## On mistakes

The bit that surprised me most: he keeps a small drawer of "spoiled"
blocks — designs where the carving went wrong somewhere on the third or
fourth motif. He doesn't throw them away. He prints with them
occasionally, on linings and inside hems, where the irregular motif sits
quietly against the more uniform outside.

"A small mistake on the inside is honest," he said. "Then the outside is
allowed to be beautiful."

I'm thinking about that for the linings on the new capsule.
